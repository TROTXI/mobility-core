Trotxi Commuter & Trotxi Driver

Your information. Clear boundaries.

Privacy notice · Updated 26 September 2026

This notice describes the Trotxi pilot commuter and driver apps and the operations website that supports them. It explains the information we use, why we use it, who can access it, and how to request deletion or ask about your information.

Driver location is for active trips, not off-duty tracking. Collection begins when a driver starts a tracked trip while the app is open. It can continue when the app is in the background or the screen is locked, and stops when the trip ends, the driver signs out, or the app confirms that the assignment is no longer authorised.

Request account or personal-data deletion without installing or signing in to the app.

Information we use

  • Identity and account: your sign-in provider identifier, name, verified email when supplied, profile details, optional phone number and optional photo. Drivers also have an operations-managed driver record, contact information, licence details, driver code and a protected PIN credential.
  • Commutes and trips: selected routes and stops, subscriptions and ride balances, reservations, boarding and no-show records, assignments, stop arrivals and trip completion.
  • Driver location: precise coordinates, accuracy, capture and receipt times during an active trip. The driver app temporarily stores unsent fixes on the device when connectivity is interrupted.
  • Payments: purchase amounts, payment references, provider outcomes, reconciliation, refund and accounting records. Paystack handles payment entry and processing; Trotxi does not store your full card number or card security code.
  • Support and communications: incident reports, work requests, support messages, relevant evidence, and delivery status for transactional emails and assignment notifications.
  • Devices and reliability: session and device-registration information, push tokens, app versions, request diagnostics, and Firebase analytics, crash and performance information where enabled. Service monitoring includes technical metrics, logs and traces.

Why we use it

We use this information to sign you in securely; plan and deliver commutes; assign vehicles and drivers; confirm boarding; account for paid rides; show entitled riders the approaching bus; process payments and refunds; send service messages; investigate incidents; prevent misuse; and diagnose reliability problems.

Driver contact emails used for onboarding and PIN recovery are delivery addresses, not proof of account ownership. A private PIN chosen by a driver is not available to operations staff. We do not sell personal information.

Location, camera and notifications

Drivers see a location disclosure before granting location access. Starting a tracked trip requires location access and location services. Android displays a location foreground-service notification; iOS may display its location indicator. Tracking is not guaranteed after force-stopping the app, rebooting the phone or losing power.

Camera access is optional, for photos or QR boarding. Drivers can use a boarding code instead of the camera. Photo-library access is used when you choose a photo. Notification permission is separate and supports service or assignment updates. You can change these permissions in device settings.

The commuter tracking screen reads the bus's position; it does not need to collect the commuter's location to display the approaching bus.

Who can access information

Authorised operations staff can access information needed for dispatch, support, safety and accounting. An assigned driver can access their trip and its manifest, including rider information needed to verify boarding. Riders entitled to a run can see its latest bus position; it is not a public driver-location feed.

We use service providers for hosting and database storage (Render), files and map delivery (Cloudflare), sign-in and mobile messaging/diagnostics (Google and Firebase; Apple when enabled), payments (Paystack), transactional email (Resend), and monitoring (Grafana). GitHub Actions runs scheduled maintenance. Providers process information necessary for their role; their infrastructure may process information outside Ghana.

Storage and retention

  • Offline driver fixes: the protected queue holds at most 1,440 fixes. Entries older than 24 hours expire when the queue is next processed. Signing out clears the device's queued coordinates.
  • Raw server traces: fixes become eligible for deletion after 30 days, except receipt-time intervals protected by an active incident evidence hold. Physical deletion requires the retention maintenance job to run.
  • Account information: kept while needed to provide the account, then scrubbed or removed through the account-erasure workflow after an approved deletion.
  • Trip, accounting, payment, security and incident history: some records remain for reconciliation, disputes, safety, fraud prevention and applicable legal obligations. These have different lifetimes from raw GPS traces; a 30-day trace policy does not mean all history disappears after 30 days. Contact us about the records relevant to your request.

Route-learning samples do not carry a trip identifier, but that alone is not a guarantee that every sample is anonymous. Deletion of account identifiers and restricted access remain important.

Your choices and requests

You can edit supported profile fields and your optional photo, manage permissions in device settings, and sign out. Commuters can initiate account deletion from Profile → Security & sign-in → Delete account. Drivers can request account and personal-data deletion through the public deletion page or operations.

You can also ask about access, corrections, retained information or a privacy concern. We verify identity before disclosing private account information or carrying out a deletion. Never send a PIN, password, access token or card details.

Contact the Trotxi privacy and deletion desk.

Changes and pilot scope

We update this notice when the service or its data practices change. The date above identifies this version. This is a description of the current pilot, not a promise that an unavailable feature is enabled or that all external cleanup finishes immediately.
